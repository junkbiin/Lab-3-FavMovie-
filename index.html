Resident Evil | From Video Game to Movie Screens

A Film By Zack Cregger

Zack Creggers's Resident Evil is a new take on video game adaptations. We are so used to adaptations failing so horribly due to the failure of capturing the essence of the game they are trying to create. Take the Minecraft movie for instance, it was a cash grab with Jack Black after his general success with the Mario Movie. It completed failed to capture the true feel of the game. But this is not the case for Resident Evil.

Zack Cregger takes a new and daring approach with this film that I believe worked incredibly well. For context, Resident Evil takes place in Raccoon City during a T-Virus outbreak that turns regualar humans into mutated monster-like zombies. Within the game, you play as a member of the specialized police task force to elimiate zombies and evacuate civilians. However in the film, Cregger explores a different story within the infected city. Instead of following the story of the police department, Cregger chooses to approach the film from the prespective of a regualar person within Raccoon City. The film follows, Bryan; a medical transport driver who is tasked to deliver a top-priority package to Raccon City General Hospital. He is reluctant to take on the task but eventually does because it pays so much more. On his journey through, he encounters many different monsters throughout. One unique thing about the movie is how relatable the main character is portrayed. Bryan is seemingly a normal guy just trying to make ends meet. This take on his character truly embodies the feeling of being a first-time player of the game.
